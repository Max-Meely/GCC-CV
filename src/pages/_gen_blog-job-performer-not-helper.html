Job performer, not helper: how to name your trade on your CV

Key points
  • Write the trade first: Civil Job Performer, not just Helper.
  • The trade links you to the right safety training and tasks.
  • Use the same title on your CV, LinkedIn and certificates.
In this articleWhy the trade mattersExamples of clear titlesAdd the company and the project

On many large oil and gas and petrochemical projects, workers are described as job performers and the trade is part of the name. A plain "Helper" or "Job Performer" tells a recruiter very little. The trade tells them where you fit.

Why the trade matters

Your job title decides which crew you join, which permits you work under and which safety training applies to you. For example: ADNOC WMS awareness, H2S awareness and permit-to-work briefings are all linked to the kind of work you do on site.

Examples of clear titles

  • Civil Job Performer: excavation, concrete, formwork and rebar support
  • Electrical Job Performer: cable pulling, tray and conduit installation support
  • Scaffolding Job Performer: assisting with scaffold erection and dismantling
  • Piping Job Performer: pipe handling, fit-up support and flange work
  • Mechanical Job Performer: equipment installation and maintenance support
  • Painting Job Performer: surface preparation and coating support
  • Insulation Job Performer: insulation and cladding support

Add the company and the project

Write the company and, if you can, the project too. For example: "Civil Job Performer | NSH | Project: Hail and Ghasha | Nov 2023 to date". The project name helps a recruiter place your experience immediately.

Tip. In our order form, choose your trade-specific role and fill in the optional Project box. We write the duties and skills to match.
Be careful. Only use a title that matches what you really did and what is written in your documents. Do not upgrade "job performer" to "technician" or "foreman".
