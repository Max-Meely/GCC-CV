What is an ATS and how to get past it

Key points
  • An ATS reads text, not design. Keep the layout simple.
  • Use standard headings like Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Use words from the job advert where they are true for you.
In this articleHow it reads your CVWhat breaks itWhat helps itDo not stuff keywords

An ATS (applicant tracking system) is software that many companies, recruiters and big contractors use to collect, read and rank CVs. If it cannot read your CV properly, a person may never see it.

How it reads your CV

The software pulls out text and tries to sort it into fields: your name, contact details, job titles, employers, dates, skills and education. Fancy layouts break this process.

What breaks it

  • Text boxes, tables and multiple complicated columns
  • Photos, logos and icons placed over the text
  • Headings with unusual names like "My Journey"
  • Information hidden in the header or footer of the file
  • Scanned images of a CV instead of real text

What helps it

  1. Use clear headings: Professional Summary, Experience, Education, Certificates, Skills.
  2. Put job title, company and dates on the same line for each job.
  3. Use a normal font such as Arial or Calibri.
  4. Save as a PDF made from a document, or as a Word file, as the advert asks.
  5. Use the exact words from the advert where they are true for you, for example the job title and the main tools or standards.
Tip. Our ATS and Technical CV is built exactly this way: plain layout, correct headings and keywords for your trade. A designed CV can still be used when a person reads it by hand.

Do not stuff keywords

Adding words you cannot back up will not help. A human will read the CV after the software, and they will ask about everything on it.
