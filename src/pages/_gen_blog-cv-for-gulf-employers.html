What a Gulf employer looks for in a CV

Key points
  • Put your target role, phone, email and location at the very top.
  • Say clearly whether you can start now or need a visa.
  • List your latest job first, with short, specific duties.
In this article1. The top of the page decides everything2. Show your status and availability3. Latest job first, with real duties4. Make your certificates easy to find5. Keep it honest and consistent

Recruiters in the UAE and wider GCC often scan a CV in well under a minute, especially when hundreds of people apply for one site or office job. Your goal is simple: make the answers to their first questions easy to find.

1. The top of the page decides everything

Start with your full name, the job you are applying for, your phone with WhatsApp, email and current location. Under that, write a short professional summary of three or four lines: your trade, your years of experience, and the kind of site or company you want to work for.

2. Show your status and availability

Employers in the Gulf need to know quickly if you can start immediately. Add your nationality, visa status and availability in one small line, for example: "Nationality: Kenyan | Visa: Employment visa | Available: Immediately".

3. Latest job first, with real duties

List your most recent job first. For each job write the job title, company, project or site, city and dates. Under it use short bullet points that describe what you actually did, not a copy of the company brochure.

  • Good: "Assisted pipe fitters with spool handling and flange alignment on a petrochemical project."
  • Weak: "Responsible for various duties as assigned."

4. Make your certificates easy to find

Site safety certificates, licences and trade tests often decide who gets called. Keep them in their own section with the name, issuer and year, so a recruiter does not have to hunt for them.

Tip. Two pages is the sweet spot for most workers and technicians. One page is fine for beginners. Anything past three pages rarely gets read.

5. Keep it honest and consistent

Dates, job titles and certificate names must match your documents. Employers in the region often verify, and a mismatch costs more than a short CV ever will.
